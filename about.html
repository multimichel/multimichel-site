It only looks like a straight line in hindsight.

I’m Michel, a Brazilian designer living in Sydney. I’ve been designing for the web since the late ’90s. On paper the route runs from early web work in Brazil to California, Booking.com in Amsterdam, Shopify in Canada, and Atlassian, Xero and Figma in Sydney. It looks much tidier written down than it ever felt living it.

How I think

What’s changed is the job title. What hasn’t is what I pay attention to. I care about the user’s actual problem, but also how it connects to the larger product, the system underneath it, and how a team makes decisions under a deadline. And I care about what finally shows up on screen once all of that gets built — the interaction, the type, the spacing, the component. Not because pixels are precious on their own, but because that’s exactly where every decision made upstream either holds up or falls apart.

At Figma

At Figma, I sit between the product and the people using it. I learn and stress-test new capabilities before they ship, work with customers to understand how their teams actually design, and bring what I learn back into the product.

That also means workshops, talks and practical guidance on design systems and AI-native workflows. The job title is different, but the work is familiar: understand the whole system, help people make better decisions, and make the product better because of it.

Besides the work

Off the clock: husband, father of three, full-time goofball, and — on the record — still available for the NBA draft, if my baseball cap collection doesn’t give the vibe away first.

I host Latinxs Who Design, a podcast about Latinx designers thriving in this industry. I teach a portfolio and career course, and I’ve mentored more than a hundred designers across ten-odd countries — through MentorCruise, ADPList, and as a Design Leadership ambassador with Echos.

I write when something has been bothering me long enough to become an argument. Most of it ends up at A List Apart or on Medium.
